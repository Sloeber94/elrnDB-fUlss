Applies to: engines: 2.2 16V JTD; all production periods; only with equipment: MY 2005

The F.A.P. system (Anti-Particle Filter) is fitted on vehicles equipped with Diesel engines in order to reduce the particles present in the exhaust gases by more than 95%.

The particulate cannot be eliminated through the use of normal catalytic converters, for this reason special (particle) filters are used which, however, suffer from the following defect: their performance deteriorates rapidly especially if used for town driving.

The F.A.P. system allows the automatic regeneration of the particle filter during the operation of the vehicle, keeping it working efficiently in all usage conditions for about 80,0000 [km] after which it should be replaced.

The FAP system consists of:

The F.A.P. system consists of:

- a silicon carbide anti-particle filter in conjunction with a catalyzer (located upstream of the filter) and sensors for checking the temperature and pressure;

- an additive system which injects an appropriate amount of cerium oxide based additive into the fuel tank each time it is refilled.

The control logic incorporated in the engine management control unit operates the artificial regeneration of the filter.

There is a diesel additive control unit that regulates the addition of additive into the fuel tank. The fuel level, engine speed and vehicle speed signals are used to carry out this operation.

Depending on the signals received, the above mentioned control unit determines the amount of additive to be injected and activates the additive injection pump.

The additive reservoir is located under the vehicle near the fuel tank: it has a capacity of 5 [l]: this quantity lasts for about 80,000 [km].

The additive injection pump, submerged in the tank, has the task of providing the pressure (3 bar) and the flow rate (80 l/h) required for the correct operation of the additive circuit.

The diesel additive level sensor has the task of notifying the control unit that the minimum level has been reached.

The temperature sensor downstream of the catalyzer (located between the catalyzer and the particle filter) allows the engine management control unit to monitor the increase in the temperature of the exhaust gases due to catalytic combustion.

The differential pressure sensor constantly measures the difference in pressure between the inlet and outlet of the catalytic converter/particle filter assembly. This measurement makes it possible to determine the filter blockage level.

The intake air heating circuit is connected to the FAP system: it involves a device designed to increase the temperature of the exhaust gases raising the temperature of the intake air. This function is only activated during the filter regeneration stage.
