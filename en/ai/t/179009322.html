Applies to: engines: 2.0 16V JTD, 2.2 16V JTD; all production periods

Description

The F.A.P. system (Anti-Particle Filter) is fitted on vehicles equipped with Diesel engines in order to reduce the particles present in the exhaust gases by more than 95%.

Particles cannot be eliminated through the use of normal catalyzers, but require the use of special filters known precisely as "anti-particle", which become prematurely unusable, especially if used for town driving.

The F.A.P. system allows the automatic regeneration of the anti-particle filter during the operation of the vehicle so that it remains efficient in all operating conditions for around 80,000 km after which the anti-particle filter should be replaced.

The FAP system consists of:

  • a silicon carbide anti-particle filter in conjunction with a catalyzer (located upstream of the filter) and sensors for checking the temperature and pressure;
  • an additive system which injects an appropriate amount of cerium oxide based additive into the fuel tank each time it is refilled.

A control logic incorporated in the engine management node, operates the artificial regeneration of the filter.

There is also a specific control unit ("additive control node") which regulates the injection of additive into the fuel tank; it uses the fuel level, engine speed and vehicle speed signals for the management of this function.

On the basis of the signals received, the additive control node:

  • determines the amount of additive to be injected,
  • activates the additive injeciton pump,
  • operates the additive injector.

The additive control node also receives a signal from the exhaust gas temperature sensor (downstream of the filter) and sends it to the engine management control unit via the body computer.

The additive reservoir is located under the vehicle near the fuel tank; it has a capacity of 5 litres (an amount which allows it to run for about 80,000 km).

The additive injection pump, submerged in the tank, has the task of providing the pressure (3 bar) and the flow rate (80 l/h) required for the correct operation of the additive circuit.

This sensor is incorporated in the pump assembly and is designed to notify the additive control node that the minimum level has been reached.

The additive injector, located on the main reservoir, allows a pre-set amount of additive to be atomized in the fuel tank; it is operated by the additive node and is an electromagnetic type injector similar to the type of injector used in petrol engines.

The temperature sensor downstream of the catalyzer, located between the catalyzer and the particle filter, allows the engine management node to monitor the temperature of the exhaust gases downstream of the catalyzer and consequently an increase in temperature caused by catalytic combustion.

The differential pressure sensor constantly measures the difference in pressure between the inlet and outlet of the catalytic converter/anti-particle filter assembly. This measurement makes it possible to determine the filter blockage level.

The intake air heating circuit is connected to the FAP system: it is a device is designed to increase the temperature of the exhaust gases by increasing the temperature of the intake air, in order to facilitate the regeneration of the particle filter (specific to the FAP device) and is only activated during the filter regeneration phase.

A throttle in the air intake is opened by a solenoid valve operated by the engine management control unit

the EGR solenoid valve [EL5050] is controlled by being totally closed or it is not operated to return it to the rest position during the heating stage.
