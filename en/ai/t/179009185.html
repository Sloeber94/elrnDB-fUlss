Applies to: engines: 2.0 16V JTD, 2.2 16V JTD; all production periods; only with equipment: Automatic Transmission and MY 2005

The ignition and recharging circuit comprises the battery, starter motor and alternator.

The (12V) lead battery requires very little maintenance.

When the ignition key is turned as far as it will go (D), the motor windings are supplied to generate the electromagnetic forces that are used to turn the starter motor pinion. This simultaneously activates the electromagnet that operates the mechanism that causes the pinion to mesh with the flywheel ring gear and thus turn the crankshaft.

On versions with automatic transmission, the enablement for the starter motor is supplied by the actual gearbox control.

See E7050 AUTOMATIC TRANSMISSION

The alternator recharges the battery during normal engine rotation.

The alternator shaft (rotor) is turned by the crankshaft via a belt: a magnetic field is produced at the fixed winding (stator) which creates an alternating current.

A diode bridge at the back of the alternator allows the alternating current to be transfored into a direct current that recharges the battery.

A voltage governor, also built into the alternator, maintains the power supply at a constant voltage (14 V) throughout the load variation and engine speed ranges.
