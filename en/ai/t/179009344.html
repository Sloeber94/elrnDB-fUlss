E5070 FAP PARTICLE FILTER SYSTEM DESCRIPTION

Applies to: engines: 2.0 16V JTD; all production periods; only with equipment: MY 2005

The F.A.P. system (Anti-Particle Filter) is fitted on vehicles equipped with Diesel engines in order to reduce the particles present in the exhaust gases by more than 95%.

Particulate cannot be removed by the use of normal catalytic converters. For this reason, special filters are used (antiparticulate), but these have the following fault: their performance drops quickly, particularly when they are used over urban routes.

The FAP system allows automatic regeneration of the antiparticulate filter during vehicle service. It keeps the filter efficient under all service conditions for approximately 80,000 km. After this, it must be replaced.

The FAP system consists of:

- a silicon carbide anti-particle filter in conjunction with a catalyzer (located upstream of the filter) and sensors for checking the temperature and pressure;

- an additive system which injects an appropriate amount of cerium oxide based additive into the fuel tank each time it is refilled.

A control logic incorporated in the engine management node, which controls artificial regeneration of the filter.

A diesel additive control unit modulates the addition of additive to the fuel tank. It uses fuel level, engine speed and vehicle speed signals to carry out this operation.

According to the signals received, the above control unit determines the amount of additive to be injected and activates the additive injection pump.

The additive tank is located under the vehicle near the fuel reservoir: its capacity is 5 [l]: this amount allows the car to cover 80,000 [km] approx.

The additive injection pump, submerged in the tank, has the task of providing the pressure (3 bar) and the flow rate (80 l/h) required for the correct operation of the additive circuit.

The diesel additive level sensor informs the control unit that the minimum level has been reached.

The temperature sensor downstream of the catalytic converter (located between the catalytic converter and particulate filter) allows the engine control unit to monitor the exhaust gas temperature increasedue to catalytic combustion.

The differential pressure sensor constantly measures the difference in pressure between the inlet and outlet of the catalytic converter/anti-particle filter assembly. This measurement makes it possible to determine the filter blockage level.

The intake air heating circuit is connected to the FAP system: this device increases the exhaust gas temperature by increasing intake air temperature. This function is activated only during the filter regeneration stage.
